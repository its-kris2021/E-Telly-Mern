# E-Telly AI Hazard Prediction — Audit & Feasibility Report

**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Scope:** Audit of E-Telly's existing AI flood/landslide prediction system and datasets, to determine whether location-specific hazard prediction is feasible without relying on citizen reports. No code was modified, no models were retrained, nothing was deployed as part of this audit.

---

## TL;DR verdict

**You already have a working two-path system, not a vaporware one** — a rule-based physics engine (PAGASA thresholds + soil moisture, zero training data) plus a trained logistic-regression ML layer (`predictionEngine.js`). The ML layer's AUC is real (0.79 flood / 0.90 landslide, honest date-based holdout, confirmed 2026-10-19) but its **precision is ~1%** — out of every ~100 AI alerts that clear the model's threshold, ~99 are false positives. This is not a bug to patch; it's the base-rate math of predicting a ~0.3–0.5%-frequency event from a training signal you don't fully trust (see below). **You can predict location-specific risk without citizen reports *for the static susceptibility component* — you're already doing it** (the NOAH/Phil-LiDAR shapefiles). You **cannot** yet predict time-varying risk independent of reports, because the one dynamic training label you have (`approved citizen report filed`) *is* a citizen-report signal by construction. Fixing that is a data problem, not an algorithm problem — swapping logistic regression for Random Forest/XGBoost will not move precision, and could make it worse by overfitting your ~90–160 positive examples.

## 1. What's actually in the codebase right now

**Two independent prediction paths**, documented in `etelly-mern/docs/HAZARD_PREDICTION_ARCHITECTURE.md` and gated against each other so they never show conflicting markers:

|                                 | Path 1 — Rule engine                                                                             | Path 2 — ML risk model ("AI")                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Files                           | `services/floodAlerts.js`, `services/landslideAlerts.js`                                      | `services/predictionEngine.js`, `services/logisticRegression.js`                                                   |
| Logic                           | PAGASA rainfall-intensity tiers (7.5/15/30 mm/hr) + soil saturation (>35%), forecast-window based | L2-regularized logistic regression (TensorFlow.js), 10 features, trained on `HazardSnapshot` + `emergency_reports` |
| Predicts                        | Whether forecast rain + current soil will physically cross a published threshold                  | Probability of an *approved citizen report* in a barangay-day                                                        |
| Needs citizen reports?          | No                                                                                                | Yes — both the training label and one input feature (`barangayReportRate30d`)                                       |
| Live AUC (honest holdout)       | n/a (not a probabilistic model)                                                                   | 0.794 flood / 0.896 landslide                                                                                          |
| Live precision (honest holdout) | n/a                                                                                               | **0.009 flood / 0.015 landslide**                                                                                |
| Live recall                     | n/a                                                                                               | 0.18 flood / 0.75 landslide                                                                                            |

Source: `scripts/output/evaluation-2026-10-19.md`, a proper **date-based holdout** (train = everything before 2025-04, val = 2025-04–2025-10, test = most recent 12 months) — not shuffled k-fold, which would leak signal across adjacent days and is why `trainRiskModel.js`'s self-reported CV numbers (~0.91 AUC) look much better than reality.

**Why precision is low** — three compounding causes, found in the code/data, not guessed:

1. **Base rate.** Positives are ~0.3–0.5% of barangay-days (22 flood / 12 landslide positives out of 5,729 test rows). Even a genuinely good classifier (AUC 0.79–0.90) produces mostly false positives at any operating point with usable recall — that's arithmetic, not a flaw to "fix" away.
2. **Label noise.** The training label is "an approved citizen report was filed," not "a hazard occurred" (`scripts/buildTrainingSet.js:137-138`). A real landslide nobody reported is a silent false negative; a barangay that over-reports minor flooding gets inflated predicted risk. No independent ground-truth incident log exists in this project.
3. **Historical data gap (now repaired).** 25,602 `HazardSnapshot` docs (Aug–Sep 2026) were missing wind/pressure/ET0/river-discharge entirely, and Dec 2025–Jul 2026 had zero snapshots before the 30-min cron started. This was fixed 2026-10-15 (`repairSnapshotFeatureGap.js` + backfill), which is what took AUC from statistically-chance (0.40/0.55) to its current 0.79/0.90 — but **a retrain to promote a model that benefits from the repaired data has not been run** ("action required, not yet done" in the architecture doc).

**Static susceptibility is already shapefile-grounded**, not hand-picked. As of 2026-10-19: `susceptibility = 1/(1+distanceKm)`, where `distanceKm` is the real distance from each of the 17 barangay centroids to the nearest "High"-class polygon in `Rizal_LandslideHazards.shp` (field `LH`, 1=Low/2=Moderate/3=High) and `Rizal_Flood_25year.shp` (field `Var`, same scale, 25-yr return period) — both sitting untracked at the repo root right now. This replaced a hardcoded 4-barangay list (`HIGH_SUSCEPTIBILITY_BARANGAYS`, still in `config/barangays.js` but now only kept as a comment reference, no longer used by the model). Method is in `scripts/computeHazardDistances.py` — point-in-polygon containment was tried first and rejected because most centroids fell in gaps between mapped patches, including barangays MGB's own high-susceptibility list names.

**Already-displayed, separate from the model:** `FloodHazardLayer.jsx` renders the **Phil-LiDAR 1/UP DREAM** WMS flood-hazard layer (`ph045802000_fh25yr_10m` — the "existing LiDAR map," already Antipolo-specific, 10m resolution, 25-yr return period), and `LandslideLayer.jsx` renders the **Philippine Geoportal/MGB** 1:10k landslide-susceptibility WMS layer (the "existing NOAH/MGB map"). These are static overlays, not fed into the ML model's live scoring — only the downloaded shapefile versions of (presumably) the same underlying data are, via the one `susceptibility` feature.

## 3. Answering the five questions directly

**(1) What additional data/features are needed for location-specific prediction?**
Two different things are being conflated in "location-specific," and the codebase already keeps them apart correctly:

- **Static susceptibility** (where is inherently hazard-prone terrain) — you have this now, shapefile-grounded, per-barangay. The ceiling here is **barangay-centroid granularity** (17 points), not sub-barangay. If you want finer-grained "where" (e.g. specific sitio/purok), you'd need either barangay-boundary polygons (don't exist in this codebase — this is why distance-to-nearest-high-zone was used instead of point-in-polygon) or a raster DEM to compute slope/flow-accumulation at finer resolution than the WMS hazard classes already give you. Not worth building without boundary polygons first.
- **Dynamic risk** (when will it happen, given current conditions) — this is where real, actionable gaps exist:
  - **An independent occurrence label.** This is the single highest-leverage missing input. Antipolo SITREPs (CDRRMO situation reports) are the most promising candidate — if they record confirmed incidents (not just citizen-filed reports), even a handful of verified true positives would let you (a) validate how much the citizen-report proxy actually tracks real occurrence, and (b) potentially retrain against ground truth instead of the proxy. Right now this gap is the actual ceiling on precision, not the algorithm.
  - **Rainfall lag features.** `rain_6h`/`rain_24h`/`rain_72h` exist but there's no multi-day antecedent-rainfall feature (e.g. 7-day cumulative), which is standard in landslide-triggering-rainfall literature (saturation builds over days, not hours).
  - **Slope/elevation per barangay**, if a DEM can be sourced (even SRTM 30m is better than nothing) — a genuinely static, report-independent signal, same category as `susceptibility` but orthogonal to it.

**(2) Do you really need MGB data? If unavailable, what alternatives?**
You don't need *more* MGB data than you already have, and what's being called "MGB data" is really two things:

- The **shapefiles** (`Rizal_LandslideHazards.shp`, `Rizal_Flood_25year.shp`) are NOAH-produced hazard classifications (NOAH's standard `LH`/`Var` schema) that are *informed by* MGB geohazard methodology — they are not a live MGB feed, and they're already integrated into the model.
- **Raw MGB susceptibility/geohazard datasets** (e.g. their 1:10,000 or 1:50,000 geohazard maps, landslide inventory points) would be the next tier up — these would allow a move from "distance to nearest polygon" toward slope-stability-class features. If MGB access is unavailable (common — MGB data requests can be slow/discretionary), the NOAH hazard maps already in hand **are the standard, defensible alternative** cited in Philippine disaster-ML literature; the Phil-LiDAR WMS layer and Philippine Geoportal WMS layer are two more free, already-wired-in alternatives available if a second susceptibility source is wanted for cross-validation.
  **Verdict: MGB is not essential.** NOAH + Phil-LiDAR + Geoportal, already in hand, cover the same ground with lower friction.

**(3) Can you combine terrain susceptibility + rainfall + historical incidents + forecasts, without citizen reports?**
Terrain susceptibility + rainfall + forecast: **yes, this is exactly Path 1** (the rule engine) — it is already citizen-report-independent by design (`floodAlerts.js`/`landslideAlerts.js`: zero report data in the decision logic) and is the component to cite when the panel asks "does it predict independent of reports?" Path 2 (ML) is where "historical incidents" currently means "historical citizen reports," because no independent incident log exists. **Path 2 cannot be made report-independent without a non-report-derived label** — that's not a modeling choice, it's a data-availability fact. If Antipolo SITREPs or MGB/NDRRMC incident logs exist with verified-occurrence timestamps, that's the one thing that would change this answer from "no" to "yes."

**(4) Improve the current algorithm, or test Random Forest/XGBoost/hybrid?**
**Improve what exists; don't swap algorithms yet.** Three independent reasons, visible in the code and data:

- Dataset size (90–160 positive examples total) is too small for tree ensembles to outperform a regularized linear model — they'd overfit the noise in a <200-example positive class, and `logisticRegression.js`'s own header comment makes this tradeoff explicit on purpose.
- The bottleneck is proven to be **label quality and base rate**, not model capacity — AUC already jumped from chance-level to 0.79–0.90 purely from a *data repair* (missing fields backfilled), with the same logistic regression. A fancier model can't out-predict a label that's a proxy for a different thing than what it's being asked to predict.
- A retrain to incorporate the Oct-15 data repair **hasn't even been run yet** on the live model (v11 flood/v9 landslide are stale, pre-repair) — that's a strictly cheaper, already-available win sitting unclaimed before any algorithm change is worth evaluating.

If alternatives are still worth testing later (e.g. once SITREP-derived ground truth exists and the dataset grows), the existing `evaluateRiskModel.js` harness (date-based holdout + bootstrap CI + rule baseline comparison + ablation support) is already built to make that comparison honestly — reuse it rather than building a new evaluation path.

**(5) Most practical approach + what to implement first?**
See prioritized plan below — short version: **retrain on already-repaired data → investigate SITREP data as a ground-truth source → decide threshold policy deliberately → only then consider algorithm changes.**

## 4. Prioritized implementation plan (no code changes made — this is the recommended order)

1. **Run `node scripts/trainRiskModel.js` to promote a model trained on the already-repaired `HazardSnapshot` data.** The live models (v11/v9) predate the Oct-15 fix; this is a zero-new-data, zero-new-code win that's been sitting ready since 2026-10-15. Needs explicit go-ahead per the promotion-safety gate already built into the script.
2. **Investigate whether Antipolo SITREPs contain a verified-occurrence timestamp independent of citizen app reports.** This is the one dataset on the list that could address the root precision ceiling (label-as-proxy), not just the symptoms. Concretely: check if a SITREP log exists with confirmed incident date/barangay, separate from `emergency_reports`. If yes, it becomes either (a) a second label source to validate the report-proxy against, or (b) a replacement label outright.
3. **Decide the threshold policy deliberately, as a recall-vs-precision tradeoff, not a default.** `findOptimalThreshold`'s `minRecall=0.15` is currently a judgment call, already flagged in-code as "revisit using fieldMetrics once a few retrain cycles accumulate." The Oct-15 audit's threshold-scan already shows the real tradeoff curve (flood recall caps at 0.182 regardless of how high the threshold is pushed; landslide degrades more smoothly) — use that curve, not intuition, to pick an operating point and document why for the panel.
4. **Run the feature-ablation pass already flagged as deferred** in the Oct-15 audit (`surfacePressure` r≈0.005-0.007, `susceptibility` r≈-0.001-0.008 — near-zero univariate correlation) using `evaluateRiskModel.js`'s existing ablation pattern, to see whether either feature is pulling its weight or just adding noise/dimensionality.
5. **Only after 1–4**, if dataset size has grown materially (more retrain cycles, possibly SITREP-sourced labels), re-evaluate Random Forest/XGBoost via the same `evaluateRiskModel.js` harness — as a comparison, not a replacement decision made in advance.
6. **Lower priority, cosmetic but panel-relevant:** confirm whether the Phil-LiDAR WMS flood layer and the downloaded `Rizal_Flood_25year.shp` are the same underlying DREAM/NOAH data under two different delivery mechanisms — if so, say so explicitly in the manuscript rather than presenting them as two independent corroborating sources.

## 5. How to validate any future precision/recall improvement honestly

Use `scripts/evaluateRiskModel.js`'s existing date-based holdout (train = oldest, val = middle 6 months for threshold selection, test = most recent 12 months) — **never** the shuffled k-fold numbers `trainRiskModel.js` prints at training time, since those leak signal across correlated nearby days and reported ~0.91 AUC when honest holdout showed as low as 0.40 before the data repair. Track the **field metrics already being persisted** (`RiskModel.fieldMetrics`: real admin confirm/reject outcomes on live AI alerts) over multiple retrain cycles — that's the ground truth for whether a change actually reduced real-world false positives, as opposed to just moving a cross-validation number.

## 6. Limitations to state plainly in the manuscript

- Precision figures (~1%) are honest and reproducible, but rest on 12–22 positive test examples per hazard — small enough that a few more/fewer real events next quarter could shift the picture noticeably. Report recall/precision with the caveat that they're estimates from a thin positive class, not settled figures.
- "No report" in the data means "no event" **or** "an event happened and nobody reported it" — indistinguishable with current data. This is the honest ceiling on the ML path's precision until an independent ground-truth source (SITREPs, if they qualify) is incorporated.
- The 17-barangay-centroid granularity is a real limit on how fine "where" can get without barangay-boundary polygon data, which doesn't exist in this codebase yet.
